Research directions

Research direction 01

Scalable Detection for Massive/XL-MIMO Networks

Massive multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO) systems offer substantial spectral-efficiency and connectivity advantages, but conventional multi-user detection can incur significant computational costs as the number of antennas grows. My research investigates randomized projection and sampling-based techniques to reduce the computational burden of detection while retaining competitive error-rate performance.

The developed algorithms exploit randomized matrix computations and multilinear signal structure, with theoretical analysis including closed-form symbol error probability expressions and probabilistic accuracy guarantees for selected methods. This line of work encompasses random-projection detectors for uplink massive MIMO and structured Kronecker random-projection techniques for XL-MIMO.

Representative contributions

  • Projection- and sampling-based low-complexity multi-user detection.
  • Analytical symbol error probability characterization and accuracy analysis.
  • Structure-aware randomized computations for massive/XL-MIMO systems.

Outcomes: J1, C3, C4, C5.

Research direction 02

Channel Estimation and Data Detection in IRS-Assisted Networks

Intelligent reflecting surfaces (IRS) offer additional degrees of freedom for wireless communications while creating computational challenges in channel estimation and data detection, particularly in large antenna and surface configurations. My research develops structured formulations and sampling-based algorithms for IRS-assisted massive/XL-MIMO networks.

For channel estimation, the work exploits multilinear structure to accelerate iterative estimation relative to conventional alternating least-squares approaches. For semi-blind data detection, sampling-based updates reduce the complexity of baseline detectors while using limited pilot information and preserving comparable detection accuracy in the studied settings.

Research direction 03

Scalable Randomized CP and Tucker Decomposition Algorithms

Tensor decomposition enables analysis of multidimensional data but is often computationally demanding at scale. My research develops randomized alternating least-squares algorithms for CP and Tucker decompositions by exploiting the Khatri–Rao and Kronecker structure in their least-squares subproblems.

The proposed volume-sampling approaches support unbiased estimation properties for selected least-squares updates, offer theoretical approximation guarantees, and demonstrate favorable empirical runtime–accuracy trade-offs against competing randomized methods, particularly at smaller sample sizes. The work also considers benefits for model averaging.
